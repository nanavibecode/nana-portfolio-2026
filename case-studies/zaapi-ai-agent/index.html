Context

The ChatGPT moment reset expectations overnight

Once ChatGPT went public in November 2022, sellers expected instant, reliable replies. Their agents were already handling around 7,000 messages a month each, and hiring more cost roughly $600 per agent per month (based on 2025 Thai salaries) — $21,000 a month for a 35-agent-equivalent team — just as Zaapi was expanding into Malaysia, Singapore, and the Philippines.

Zaapi had seen the trend coming: engineering was already prototyping AI features. So the question was never whether to ship AI. It was how to make it trustworthy enough that a brand would let it talk to its own customers.

Problem

Three groups, one trust problem

Before replying, agents had to hunt for context across different tools, which slowed replies and made answers inconsistent. AI could fix that — but each group worried about a different risk.

Customer Service Manager

Pain points
  • Training new agents doesn't scale
  • Inconsistent tone across many agents
  • Fears AI that's inaccurate or out of control — and has no time to set it up

Chat Agent

Pain points
  • Switching tabs to find basic context
  • Rewriting similar replies, unsure of tone or policy
  • Fears giving wrong info, with no way to check the source

End Customer

Pain points
  • Needs an answer now
  • Gets replies that don't actually help
  • Left unsure what happens next

My Role

What I owned, end to end

Engineering could already build the AI. My job was to turn it into something customer service teams would trust enough to switch on — and prove with numbers that they did. As Zaapi's only product designer (Senior, promoted to Lead along the way), I owned the in-production experiments, intent mapping, interviews with managers running AI, the success metrics, how the AI shows its reasoning, the testing environment, the performance dashboard, and handover. I worked with the Head of Product, five engineers, and data, meeting weekly on one shared set of numbers. Design Process

Learn from real usage, not a lab

Engineering already had working prototypes, so instead of a long discovery phase, we learned from real usage:

  • Ship small, then tweak — a lightweight beta in production, improved with quick A/B-style changes.
  • Rank what matters most — reviewing real chats from our heaviest users. For one large team, order status alone was 40% of all chats.
  • Talk to the people running AI — interviews with managers overseeing AI agents and experienced AI users.

What managers told us they needed

  • Quality they can check — they were already reviewing AI replies by hand, and wanted to see the AI's reasoning and sources.
  • A way to improve it — one place to see what the AI couldn't answer, and what to add.
  • Numbers they can report — chats AI resolved fully or partly, time saved, and AI vs. human volume.

Those became our success measures — with messages sent by AI as the main one, because it tracks directly to revenue.

Key Decisions

What I chose, and why

Four decisions shaped whether teams would trust the AI enough to use it.

Ship a draft button before shipping an agent

Alternative: launching a fully autonomous AI chatbot from day one

AI 1.0 (Sep 2023) only drafted replies for an agent to review and send. AI was still unreliable, and a wrong answer in a brand's name costs more trust than a slow one. There was no outside pressure — it was a pure experiment, and its first question was simply: would anyone use it?

Fix discoverability before building more AI

Alternative: adding more AI features while the beta sat unused

The button had been in the message box from day one, but as a small, unlabeled icon, nobody knew it was there. We turned it into a big button with clear copy — "AI response" — and validated it in the live app. Daily AI use went from about 10 to 85 events: +750%, without changing the AI at all.

Show the reasoning, not just the answer

Alternative: black-box replies — a cleaner UI with less to explain

Managers were already checking AI replies by hand, so trust had to come from being able to check the AI's work. Every answer shows its source and how up to date it is, an "AI thinking" view shows each step, and teams can test the agent before it talks to customers. That's what gave teams the confidence to let AI reply: messages sent by AI grew +100% in two quarters.

One toggle to deploy — then outgrowing it

Alternative: setting AI up in its own module vs. as a step inside the Flow Builder

We tested both and launched with the simplest: one page, one on/off toggle, so busy managers could go live fast. It worked for getting started but gave larger teams too little control, so setup moved into the Flow Builder: trigger → conditions → "Let AI Agent handle."

Solution & Delivery

A ladder of trust, from draft to action

Each step gives the AI more responsibility — so each one had to earn the team's trust first.

01 · Draft

02 · Copilot

AI shows its work

AI replies — and knows when to stop

04 · Actions · 2026

AI does the next step

AI Agent 2.0: one agent per brand that can cancel orders, approve refunds, and connect to other platforms.

Has to prove: accuracy high enough to act, not just answer.

01 — Draft

AI 1.0 drafted a reply from the customer's latest message, set up from a single FAQ document.

02 — Copilot

Teams teach the AI with knowledge sources, a system prompt, personality, and scenarios. The "AI thinking" view shows how it works through a question — think, act (look up knowledge or pick a scenario), observe, respond — and a test environment lets teams try it safely before going live.

03 — Autopilot

Teams can let the agent reply on its own. When it isn't confident, it asks a clarifying question or hands the chat to a human. The performance dashboard shows every chat AI touched, how many it resolved fully or partly, and the time and cost saved.

Handover

Documented in Figma with states, edge cases, and interaction notes — so engineering could build the reasoning loop and every fallback state without guessing.

04 — Actions: AI Agent 2.0

In 2026, AI Agent 2.0 restructures the AI around one agent per brand. Like a human brand representative, it holds the brand's knowledge, procedures, and actions in one place — and it can act: cancel orders, approve cancellations and refunds, and send and receive data with other platforms through APIs, with new features focused on accuracy. It's rebuilt on a new architecture:

TechniqueHow it worksWhat it means for the design
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Before answering, the AI looks up the brand's own information — website, documents, order details — and answers from that, not from general knowledge. Replies can show their source, and unanswered questions show the team what to add.
Reranking The AI's search brings back many possible matches; reranking keeps only the few that best answer the customer's question. Fewer confident-but-wrong answers, and the reasoning view shows only what the AI actually used.
Agentic tool calling The AI can use tools, not just write — look up an order, cancel an order, approve a refund, or connect to other platforms. Every action needs permission, a clear confirmation, a record, and a safe fallback.
Multi-agent architecture Instead of one AI doing everything, several specialist AIs each handle one job — understanding, finding information, acting, checking safety — with a coordinator between them. It should feel like one assistant to the customer, while the team can see which part did what.
Model routing and multi-provider inference Simple questions go to fast, low-cost AI models and harder ones to stronger models, across more than one AI provider so it keeps running if one goes down. Whichever model answers, the brand voice, tone, and rules must stay the same.

Every action raises the cost of a wrong answer — so the trust design from version one (visible reasoning, confidence thresholds, a human who can always step in) matters even more in 2.0.

Where it stands today — Q3 2026

From 1 July to 29 September 2026:

  • 1 in 20 replies sent through Zaapi now comes from AI (5.2%), rising from 4.6% in July to 5.7% in September. That's 1.67M AI replies in the quarter, +68% vs. Q2 (0.99M). Counting replies sellers type directly in the marketplace apps too, AI is 2.6% of all seller-side messages.
  • 13% of all closed tickets were resolved end to end by AI (586K of 4.49M), up from 10% in Q2, and 17% in September. Of the tickets the AI worked on, 61% were fully resolved with no human — steady vs. Q2, and 64% in September.
  • 240 stores ran the AI agent in September, up from 165 in April (+45%) — roughly 1 in 4 of Zaapi's 968 active stores. 382 different stores used it at some point in the quarter.

"Replies through Zaapi" are messages sent by seller agents, flows, automations, and AI in Zaapi, excluding buyer messages and replies typed in Shopee's or Lazada's own apps. "Full resolution" means a ticket closed with AI handling it and no human reply; "partial" means AI replied, then a human stepped in (376K tickets in Q3). "Running the AI agent" means at least one ticket resolved fully or partly by AI that month. September data runs to 29 Sep, so final quarter figures will be slightly higher.

What worked

Three things made it work. Start small and move fast, with one shared set of numbers across design, engineering, and data. Build trust by showing the source, and let teams test before going live. Make performance visible, so teams could see what the AI was doing and improve it themselves.

What I'd improve

Moving fast left two kinds of debt. We tied AI training sources to each account — quick to ship, but hard to reuse across teams or brands. And the one-toggle setup got people started but gave larger teams too little control. Both came from optimizing the first release for speed without first designing what an "agent" is as a system. AI Agent 2.0 fixes that by making the agent itself the core object. The lesson I carry forward: keep the first release simple on the surface, but design the model underneath from day one — renaming a button is cheap; rebuilding the foundation isn't.

Why we're doubling down

The results earned a second chapter: AI became Zaapi's top revenue-generating feature, AI replies grew 68% in a single quarter, and AI agents are where customer service software is heading. That's why 2026 is about AI Agent 2.0 — an agent that can act like a human teammate, not just answer like one.

Executive Summary

After ChatGPT launched publicly and went viral in November 2022, sellers expected instant replies, and each support agent already handled about 7,000 messages a month across Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop, LINE, Facebook, and Instagram.
The Problem
Hiring doesn't scale, but AI without guardrails makes things up and costs brands trust. And the managers who'd switch it on had no time to set it up.

Context

ChatGPT reset expectations overnight

From late 2022, sellers expected instant, reliable replies. Each new agent cost about $600 a month (2025 Thai salaries), just as Zaapi was expanding into Malaysia, Singapore, and the Philippines.

Engineering was already prototyping AI, so the question was never whether to ship it — but how to make it trustworthy enough for a brand to let it talk to its customers.

Problem

Three groups, one trust problem

Agents hunted for context across tools, so replies were slow and inconsistent. AI could help — but each group feared a different risk.

Customer Service Manager

Pain points
  • Training agents doesn't scale
  • Inconsistent tone across the team
  • Fears AI going wrong — no time to set it up

Chat Agent

Pain points
  • Switching tabs for basic context
  • Rewriting the same replies
  • Fears giving wrong info, can't check the source

End Customer

Pain points
  • Needs an answer now
  • Gets replies that don't help
  • Unsure what happens next

My Role

What I owned, end to end

Engineering could build the AI. My job was to make teams trust it enough to switch it on — and prove it with numbers. As Product Design Lead, I owned:

  • Research — in-production experiments, intent mapping, interviews with managers running AI
  • Trust design — visible reasoning and sources, the testing environment, human handoff
  • Measurement — the success metrics and the AI performance dashboard
  • Delivery — handover, with a weekly sync with the Head of Product, 5 engineers, and data on one shared set of numbers

Design Process

Learn from real usage, not a lab

Prototypes already existed, so we skipped long discovery and learned in production:

  • Ship small, then tweak — a light beta, improved with quick A/B-style changes
  • Rank by real chats — for one large team, order status alone was 40% of all chats
  • Talk to managers running AI — and to experienced AI users

What managers needed

  • Quality they can check — the AI's reasoning and sources
  • A way to improve it — what the AI couldn't answer, and what to add
  • Numbers they can report — chats resolved, time saved, AI vs. human volume

These became our success measures. The main one: messages sent by AI, because it tracks directly to revenue.

Key Decisions

What I chose, and why

1. A draft button before an agent

Instead of: a fully autonomous chatbot from day one

AI 1.0 (Sep 2023) only drafted replies for a person to send. A wrong answer in a brand's name costs more than a slow one, so we framed it as an experiment with one question: would anyone use it?

2. Fix discoverability before building more AI

Instead of: adding features while the beta sat unused

The button was a small, unlabeled icon nobody noticed. We made it a clear "AI response" button and tested it live. Daily use went from about 10 to 85 — +750%, with no change to the AI.

3. Show the reasoning, not just the answer

Instead of: black-box replies with a cleaner UI

Managers were already checking replies by hand. So every answer shows its source, an "AI thinking" view shows each step, and teams can test before going live. Messages sent by AI then grew +100% in two quarters.

4. One toggle to deploy — then outgrowing it

Instead of: setup inside the Flow Builder from the start

We tested both and launched the simplest: one page, one toggle, so busy managers went live fast. Larger teams needed more control, so setup later moved into the Flow Builder: trigger → conditions → "Let AI Agent handle."

A ladder of trust, from draft to action

Each step gives AI more responsibility — and had to earn the team's trust first.

04 · Actions · 2026

AI does the next step

Has to prove: accurate enough to act.

01 — Draft

AI 1.0 drafted a reply from the customer's latest message, using one FAQ document.

02 — Copilot

Teams teach the AI with knowledge sources, a system prompt, personality, and scenarios. "AI thinking" shows each step — think, act, observe, respond — and a test environment lets teams try it safely first.

03 — Autopilot

AI replies on its own. When unsure, it asks a clarifying question or hands off to a human. The dashboard shows every chat AI touched, what it resolved, and the time and cost saved.

Handover

Figma specs with states, edge cases, and interaction notes — so engineering could build every fallback without guessing.

04 — Actions: AI Agent 2.0

In 2026, 2.0 rebuilds the AI as one agent per brand — holding its knowledge, procedures, and actions, like a human brand representative. It can cancel orders, approve refunds, and exchange data with other platforms. What's under the hood, and what it means for the design:

TechniqueIn plain wordsWhat it means for the design
RAGAnswers from the brand's own data, not general knowledge.Replies show their source; gaps show what to add.
RerankingKeeps only the few best matches from a search.Fewer confident-but-wrong answers.
Agentic tool callingUses tools — look up, cancel, refund, connect.Every action needs permission, confirmation, a record, and a fallback.
Multi-agentSpecialist AIs with a coordinator.Feels like one assistant; the team sees which part did what.
Model routingEasy questions to fast models, hard ones to stronger, across providers.Same brand voice and rules, whichever model answers.

Acting raises the cost of a wrong answer — so the trust design from 1.0 matters even more in 2.0.

Where it stands today — Q3 2026

  • 1.67M AI replies, +68% vs. Q2 (0.99M) — rising from 4.6% of replies in July to 5.7% in September.
  • 586K of 4.49M tickets closed fully by AI (17% in September). Of tickets AI worked on, 61% needed no human (64% in September).
  • 382 stores used the AI agent at some point in the quarter, out of 968 active stores.

Data: 1 Jul – 29 Sep 2026; final figures slightly higher. "Replies through Zaapi" excludes buyer messages and replies typed in Shopee's or Lazada's own apps (counting those, AI is 2.6% of seller messages). "Full resolution" = closed with no human reply; "partial" = AI replied, then a human stepped in (376K tickets). "Running" = at least one AI-resolved ticket that month.

What worked

Start small, move fast, on one shared set of numbers. Earn trust by showing the source and letting teams test first. Make performance visible, so teams improve the AI themselves.

What I'd improve

Speed left debt: training sources tied to each account, hard to reuse, and a one-toggle setup too simple for larger teams. Both came from shipping before defining what an "agent" is as a system — which 2.0 now fixes. The lesson: keep the first release simple on the surface, but design the model underneath from day one. Renaming a button is cheap; rebuilding the foundation isn't.

What's next

The results earned a second chapter: AI Agent 2.0 — an agent that can act like a teammate, not just answer like one.
